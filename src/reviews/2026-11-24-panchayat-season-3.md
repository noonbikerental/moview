Three seasons in, **Panchayat** could easily have coasted on Phulera nostalgia—chai under the neem tree, lauki gifts, and Abhishek Tripathi's reluctant attempts to crack his CAT exam. Instead, Season 3 shifts the series onto trickier terrain, letting local rivalries turn genuinely bitter without losing its gentle comic rhythm.

## A Village Election Casts a Longer Shadow

Following the emotional fallout at the end of Season 2, the opening episodes take their time rebuilding daily life in Phulera. The arrival of a replacement sachiv triggers petty turf wars, while MLA Chandra Kishore Singh's bruised ego escalates into real intimidation around highway construction and housing schemes.

Jitendra Kumar remains wonderfully understated as Abhishek—less detached than in Season 1, yet still torn between his metropolitan ambitions and the people he has grown protective of. Raghubir Yadav, Neena Gupta, Faisal Malik, and Chandan Roy continue to form one of the tightest ensembles in Indian television, while Durgesh Kumar's Bhushan sharpens into an effective foil.

## Where the Pacing Wobbles

With eight brisk episodes, a couple of mid-season subplots—particularly around the pigeon release and village road politics—stretch a thin joke slightly past its natural landing. Yet whenever writer Chandan Kumar pivots back to Prahlad's quiet grief or Manju Devi's reluctant leadership, the writing regains immediate emotional clarity.

## Final Word

**Panchayat** Season 3 is slightly heavier and less breezy than its debut outing, but its empathy for small-town life remains unmatched on Prime Video.
